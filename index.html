The Problem

Surface clean-ups can't see the seabed

Waste segregation, disposal and sanitation efforts routinely stop at the waterline. Ghost nets, plastic, tyres and drums accumulate unseen on harbour floors and riverbeds — degrading marine ecosystems, entangling wildlife, and quietly re-entering the food chain. There is no systematic, low-cost way for port authorities and fisheries departments to find, prioritise and recover this hidden waste at scale.

Our Solution

One pipeline — from sonar ping to recycler handover

JalNiriksh AI combines side-scan sonar analysis with a 3D ocean map to rank underwater items for recovery, then routes them through on-deck segregation and recycler handover — with a seabed cleanliness index to track every harbour over time.

How It Works

User flow — from boat scan to dispatch report

A boat scans the harbour floor with sonar. Seven steps turn that raw scan into a prioritised, trackable recovery operation.

  1. 1

    Upload the scan

    Upload the sonar file, or let the boat send it automatically.

  2. 2

    AI checks the images

    Cleans up the grainy sonar picture, spots things that look like waste (nets, plastic, tyres, drums), ignores rocks, sand ripples and shadows, and gives each item a confidence score and GPS spot on the map.

  3. 3

    A person double-checks

    Items the AI was unsure about (40–80% confidence) get a close-up photo for an analyst to confirm or reject. The observation is saved to retrain the model.

  4. 4

    See it on the 3D map

    Each confirmed item sits on the seabed at its real depth, with an accuracy circle, shaded survey-coverage strips, and a priority based on potential economic and ecological harm.

  5. 5

    Plan and go collect

    The app plans the shortest boat route and sends GPS points to the boat. Divers go for shallow items; an underwater robot handles deep ones.

  6. 6

    Sort and dispose

    Waste collected is sorted and disposed of safely for hand-off to recyclers.

  7. 7

    Report

    The port sees how clean each area is and where to scan next, then the cycle starts again from step one.

Technical Approach

Under the hood

1 · Sonar Ingestion & Clean-up

Reads side-scan sonar files (XTF/JSF) with their GPS data. Corrects image geometry, evens out brightness, reduces grainy noise, then tiles the scan for analysis.

2 · Detection & Triage

YOLO segmentation, an anomaly check and a shadow baseline are combined. False positives are filtered, each item gets a calibrated score, and uncertain items go to human review.

3 · Location & 3D Map

Items get a GPS position, depth and an error circle, with duplicates merged, shown on a CesiumJS map alongside currents, coverage and depth.

4 · Recovery & Segregation

Items are ranked by hazard and routed with OR-Tools. Recovered waste is sorted on deck for recyclers, and every outcome retrains the model.

Feasibility & Viability

Built on a proven, low-cost stack

Technical Feasibility

  • Proven open-source stack: YOLO segmentation for sonar, xarray + ERDDAP for ocean data, CesiumJS for the 3D map — all mature and free.
  • Standard data, real sources: reads XTF/JSF sonar and NetCDF ocean files; trained on public sonar data and synthetic ghost nets, tested on real scans.

Economic Feasibility

  • Government & B2B model: annual licences for port authorities, state fisheries departments and pollution control boards, at an estimated ₹13–20L per deployment.
  • Low running cost: reuses sonar surveys ports already run; works offline on a single GPU laptop on the survey boat.

Solution Viability

  • Modular architecture: detection, 3D map, prioritisation and recovery planning can each be deployed on their own.
  • Improves with use: analyst checks and what crews actually recover become new training labels for the AI.
  • Coasts and rivers: the same pipeline adapts to survey rivers like the Ganga, not just harbours.

Potential Risks & Solutions

⚡ Noisy sonar images

Sonar clean-up, a false-positive filter, and human review of uncertain detections.

🕸️ Little ghost-net training data

Public sonar datasets plus synthetic nets, with field-verified labels added over time.

📡 Sonar can't identify material

Sonar gives a likely category; the material is confirmed and weighed on deck before disposal.

Impact & Benefits

Why this matters

  • Hidden waste: the Ganges carries an estimated ~120,000 tonnes of plastic to the sea every year.
  • Livelihoods at stake: India's fishing sector supports an estimated 14.5 million livelihoods.
  • Recycling route exists: India's 2022 waste-tyre EPR rules give recovered tyres a regulated recycling route.
  • Segregation by design: recovered items are sorted and weighed on deck by material, then handed to recyclers with a manifest.
  • Measurable sanitation: our proposed index tracks harbour cleanliness as confirmed items per km² surveyed.
